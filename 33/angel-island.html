Angel Island Campout

Overnight Expedition · Summer Solstice

Dates
Sun, June 21 – Mon, June 22
Depart SF
2:05 PM Ferry
SF Ferry Building, Gate B
Campground
North Garrison Group

We’re camping out overnight on Angel Island to celebrate Dixon’s Trenta-y-Tresiñera and the summer solstice. We can accommodate up to 30 people in the North Garrison Group campground — tucked above the historic U.S. Immigration Station on the north side of the island, with sweeping views of Marin, the Bay, and the city skyline.

It’s the longest day of the year, on a 740-acre island in the middle of the Bay, with no cars and no last ferry to catch. Scroll down for the practical stuff — it’s a real campout, not a glamp.

Bring WARM layers. June is statistically the windiest month on Angel Island. Daytime is 60s–70s, but nights drop to the low 50s and the fog rolls in fast. Bring more warmth than you think you need.

Getting There & Hauling Gear

  • The North Garrison campground is about 0.8 miles from the Ayala Cove ferry dock, with roughly 300 ft of elevation gain. Paved road most of the way.
  • Plan on 20 minutes to walk up with your stuff. We’ll regroup at the dock and head up together.
  • Bikes and wagons are allowed and very much used — people haul up full coolers, suitcases, the works. A folding wagon for shared loads is gold.
  • No shuttle service to the campsites. Whatever you bring, you carry (or roll). Think backpacking backpacks, or wagons.
  • The ferry ticket includes the state park entrance fee. Hang on to it.

What to Pack

Stay Warm
  • Fleece or puffy + a windbreaker / shell
  • Beanie — seriously, the wind
  • Long pants and warm socks for evening
  • Sleeping bag rated for ~40°F (or warmer with a liner)
  • Insulated sleeping pad
Shelter & Light
  • Sturdy tent — bring extra stakes and guy lines
  • Headlamp + spare batteries
  • Optional: small lantern for the picnic table
Daytime
  • Sturdy walking shoes
  • Sunscreen + sunglasses — the day sun is real
  • Refillable water bottle (potable water at camp)
  • Day pack for hikes to the summit or perimeter loop

At Camp

  • Picnic tables, food lockers, and a charcoal BBQ on site
  • Potable running water spigot — refill bottles freely
  • Pit toilets nearby (bring your own TP just in case)
  • Cell service is actually decent across most of the island
  • No wood fires — charcoal or camp stoves only. There’s no charcoal for sale on the island, so bring what you need.
  • No dogs (service animals OK). No swimming — strong currents, no lifeguards.
  • Travel after sunset is restricted in parts of the island, so plan to be at camp by dark.
About the raccoons. Angel Island raccoons are bold, organized, and deeply unimpressed by your snacks. Lock everything — food, sunscreen, toothpaste, lip balm, anything scented — in the food lockers any time you walk away. Never leave food in your tent.
